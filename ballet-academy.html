Understanding the Academy

What Is the Ballet Academy?
A Clear Guide for Parents.

If your child has fallen in love with dance in a once-a-week recreational class, you may wonder what serious classical ballet training looks like. The Ballet Academy of Chapel Hill is our pre-professional classical conservatory operating under the umbrella of Renner Dance.

Rather than learning a single routine for an end-of-year recital, Academy dancers follow a structured, multi-day classical syllabus in Ballet Technique, Pointe, Classical Variations, and Pilates Conditioning. Every class builds proper posture, turnout, foot articulation, and musicality step by step.

Designed for flexibility, students may enroll exclusively in the Ballet Academy as classical specialists, or pair their Academy training with Renner Dance Company to combine conservatory-level ballet with versatility across lyrical, contemporary, jazz, and tap.

Program Architecture

How the Ballet Academy Works

Four distinct, complementary pillars that form a complete classical education.

Technique & Lineage

Cecchetti & Vaganova Methods

Dancers train in two of the world's most respected classical systems. The Italian Cecchetti method develops clean footwork, anatomical purity, and centered balance, while the Russian Vaganova method builds expressive upper-body port de bras, dynamic turns, and powerful elevation.

Multi-Day Weekly Technique Syllabus
Strength & Artistry

Pointe & Classical Variations

Progression onto pointe is never rushed. Dancers build foundational ankle, foot, and core alignment in Levels A and B before advancing to dedicated weekly Pointe classes and historic classical repertoire variations in Levels C and D under direct faculty supervision.

Readiness-Based Pointe Advancement
Biomechanics & Safety

Pilates & Ballet Conditioning

Classical artistry requires athletic durability. Integrated Pilates and ballet-specific floor conditioning strengthen deep core stabilizers, hip turnout muscles, spinal alignment, and safe flexibility so young dancers grow stronger while preventing overuse injuries.

Built Into Weekly Academy Training
Program Integration

Academy-Only or Paired with Company

Students may enroll solely in the Ballet Academy (2 to 3 days per week by level) or combine Academy training with Renner Dance Company. Company members take additional days of Academy ballet alongside their Company classes for up to four nights of ballet weekly.

Placement by Evaluation with Ms. Rossana
Why Classical Training Matters

What the Ballet Academy Does for Your Child

Whether your child dreams of a professional ballet career or simply wants to become the strongest, most poised version of themselves, conservatory ballet training delivers lifelong benefits that no other activity replicates.

Physical Development

Posture, Alignment & Injury-Free Strength

Classical training teaches a child how to hold their spine, engage their core, and align their joints with anatomical precision. Through disciplined barre work and Pilates conditioning, dancers develop remarkable balance, coordination, and body awareness that protect growing bones and muscles.

Cross-Style Mastery

The Technical Engine for Every Style of Dance

Ballet is the universal grammar of movement. A dancer who masters classical line, extension, spotting, and foot articulation in the Ballet Academy immediately elevates their performance in lyrical, contemporary, jazz, and musical theatre, standing out effortlessly in auditions and competitions.

Mind & Character

Mental Focus, Composure & Quiet Confidence

In a world of instant gratification, classical ballet teaches children the deeply rewarding discipline of incremental mastery. Translating complex combinations and classical music into fluid movement sharpens memory, focus, and resilience, building calm poise both on stage and in the classroom.

Future Doors Opened

Preparation for Elite Conservatories, Universities & Companies

Training under internationally decorated faculty gives ambitious dancers authentic classical pedigree. Our students are prepared to audition confidently for national summer intensives, university BFA dance programs, collegiate dance teams, and world-renowned professional ballet academies.

Classical Corps & Ensemble Precision Stage Repertoire

Structured Progression

Levels A, B, C & D

Dancers are placed into one of four progressive levels based on age, technical readiness, and experience. All placements are determined at Ms. Rossana's discretion, with mid-season evaluations in January.

Level A
Foundational Academy
2 Days / Week

Our entry-level Academy tier for dancers ready to commit to serious classical training. Builds strong postural alignment, turnout, and foundational barre and center work.

✦2 days per week (Mondays & Fridays)
✦Foundational Cecchetti & Vaganova technique
✦Pre-pointe foot & ankle strengthening
✦Ballet-specific conditioning & alignment
✦Nutcracker & Spring Showcase opportunities
Level B
Intermediate Academy
2 Days / Week

✦2 days per week (Mondays & Fridays)

Stage Experience

Performance Opportunities

Classical training culminates on stage. Every Ballet Academy student is invited to perform in two major classical productions each season, building theatrical presence and confidence in front of live audiences.

December · Holiday Tradition

The Nutcracker

Our annual winter production of The Nutcracker gives Ballet Academy dancers the opportunity to perform in one of classical ballet's most beloved full-stage traditions, from corps de ballet ensembles to demi-soloist and principal variations.

Rehearsals Begin the week of August 25; two Saturdays per month (Sept to Dec) plus Thanksgiving week rehearsal
Eligibility All Ballet Academy levels (Levels A, B, C & D)
Stage Focus Classical character, corps precision & solo repertoire
December Stage Production
May · End-of-Season Production

Spring Academy Showcase

Held every May, the Spring Academy Showcase celebrates a full season of technical growth. Dancers present classical repertoire, original choreography, and variations that highlight their progression in technique and pointe work.

Rehearsals Conducted directly during weekly Ballet Academy class time with no extra weekend rehearsals
Eligibility All Ballet Academy levels (Levels A, B, C & D)
Stage Focus Classical repertoire, pointe variations & ensemble artistry
May Stage Production
Invitation-Only Opportunity

Competitive Variations Track

Select Ballet Academy dancers are evaluated and invited at Ms. Rossana's discretion to learn and compete historic classical variations, contemporary solos, and classical ensemble works on the international stage.

Evaluation & Selection

Invitation by the Ballet Mistress

Participation in the Competitive Variations Track is by invitation at Ms. Rossana Nesta's discretion. Dancers are selected based on technical readiness, work ethic in weekly Academy classes, and readiness to represent the Ballet Academy in solo classical variations or ballet ensembles.

Classical & Contemporary Categories

Ballet Academy Faculty

Led by Ballet Mistress Rossana Nesta alongside Assistant Director Rachel Carr and faculty instructor Chaveli Peña, our faculty brings authentic European conservatory lineage and professional company experience to Chapel Hill.

Ballet Mistress · Ballet Academy Director

Rossana Nesta

Cecchetti & Vaganova Methods · Classical Repertoire · Variations · Pilates

Born in Rome, Rossana Nesta graduated from the prestigious Balletto di Roma, where she taught from 1987 to 1995, and earned her Bachelor in Teaching Ballet in 1989 from the Accademia Nazionale di Danza in Rome, the highest level of classical certification sponsored by the Italian Ministry of Public Instruction.

She completed advanced pedagogical training in Varna, Bulgaria during the International Ballet Competition and at the Carcano Theater in Milan under Anna Maria Prina, Director of the Teatro alla Scala Ballet School. After moving to the United States in 1996, she taught for five years at Pittsburgh Ballet Theatre and Point Park University, and in 2002 became a Partner of the School of American Ballet (SAB) in New York.

In North Carolina, Ms. Rossana served as Ballet Mistress at Cary Ballet Conservatory, performed professionally with Carolina Ballet, and founded the International Ballet Academy, where she received a license from the George Balanchine Foundation to stage New York City Ballet repertoire. Since 2019, she has led the Ballet Academy of Chapel Hill at Renner Dance, mentoring students with the discipline and care that have propelled her dancers into the world's leading companies.

Ballet Academy Assistant Director

Rachel Carr

Classical Ballet · Pointe · Variations Coaching · Conditioning

Rachel Carr grew up training and performing classical repertoire with The Fairfax Ballet in Virginia before earning her Bachelor of Arts in Dance Performance with a concentration in Ballet from Point Park University's acclaimed Conservatory of Performing Arts in Pittsburgh.

As Assistant Director of the Ballet Academy of Chapel Hill, Ms. Rachel works hand in hand with Ms. Rossana across every level of the conservatory. She co-directs private variations coaching for our YAGP, UBC, and ADC competitors, teaches weekly Academy technique and pointe classes, choreographs for Academy showcases, and coordinates family communications and scheduling.

B.A. Dance Performance (Ballet) Point Park University The Fairfax Ballet Variations & Pointe Specialist

Ballet Academy Faculty

Chaveli Peña

Classical Ballet · Repertoire · Contemporary & Lyrical

Chaveli Peña trained directly in the classical conservatory tradition at the International Ballet Academy and in the pre-professional division at the School of Carolina Ballet, where her technical excellence earned her promotion to Apprentice with Carolina Ballet.

On stage, Ms. Chaveli has performed leading and featured classical roles in Giselle, The Nutcracker, and Don Quixote, as well as contemporary ballet repertoire by Carolina Ballet Artistic Director Zalman Raffael. Having grown up within this exact classical training lineage, she brings both technical precision and inspiring mentorship to our young dancers, helping them bridge rigorous ballet technique with expressive contemporary artistry.
